Safety Awareness

In her honor — so every child comes home

This page is here for one reason: Zanaiah's family wants every young person to make it home safe. These are simple words, shared with love — for kids, for teens, and for the parents reading beside them.

Why This Page Exists

A few honest words from her family.

Our family lost Zanaiah in a motor vehicle accident. Nothing can change that for us, and no family should ever have to carry what we carry.

But if her story helps even one young person pause, listen, and choose the safer way home, then something good has come from her name. That is our hope for this page — that it protects another child, and spares another family.

Read it together. Talk about it at home. Simple things save lives, and they matter more than anyone knows — until they do.

Listen to Your Parents

The people who love you most are trying to protect you.

Your parents' rules are not there to ruin your fun, and they are not there because they don't trust you. They come from love — and from a lifetime of seeing how fast one ordinary moment can change everything.

When a parent says be careful, call me, or not tonight, hear what is underneath it: I need you to come home.

It is never weak to listen. It is never embarrassing to be careful. The strongest young people are the ones who hear the people who love them most — and take it to heart.

Choose Your Circle

Real friends want you to get home safe.

The people around you shape the choices in front of you. Stay close to the ones who look out for you — and keep your distance from the wrong influence, even when stepping back feels hard.

If someone pressures you toward danger — toward speed, toward showing off, toward anything you already know isn't right — that person is not thinking about you in that moment. Real friends never ask you to risk your life to prove something.

You can walk away. You can say no. You can be the one who leaves. That doesn't make you less than anyone — it makes you the one who chose wisely, and there is nothing braver than that.

Stay Safe Out There

Small habits, every single time.

  • Wear your seatbelt — every ride, every time, even the short ones. Most trips feel routine right up until the moment they aren't.
  • Never ride with an impaired or reckless driver. If a driver has been drinking or using anything, or drives like the road is a game, do not get in — and if you're already in, ask to be let out somewhere safe. Call a parent instead. There is no shame in that call, ever.
  • Be careful around motorcycles and vehicles. Give them room, watch before you step or turn, and never assume a driver or rider has seen you.
  • Put the phone away near roads. Look up, look both ways, and keep looking until you're across.
  • Be seen at night. Light colors, a light on your phone or your bike, and someone at home who knows where you are and when you'll be back.

If It Doesn't Feel Right

Trust the feeling. Make the call.

If something feels wrong — a ride, a plan, a crowd, a moment — trust that feeling. You don't have to explain it, you don't have to prove it, and you don't have to stay.

Tell a trusted adult. Call home. Whatever the hour, whatever the situation: a parent would rather get a call asking for help than any other call in this world. Asking for help is always, always allowed.

From Her Family

Hold your children close. And young people — hold on to the voices that love you. They are trying to bring you home.
